Water Security Futures Index · proposal for discussion

One formula, worked on real data

A proposed way to turn the five pillars into one score per country. The score runs from 0 (prepared) to 100 (least prepared), and 50 is the point where risk equals readiness.

This is a mockup. Every number on the map is computed from real data in the WSFI database, but pillars A and C do not have their own indicators yet. A uses two stand-in measures of today's water pressure and C is an estimate. The table further down shows what is real and what is illustrative.

The proposed formula

Scale each data point

x → 0 … 1

Fixed limits per data point, so a country's score does not move when other countries change.

Average into pillars

A, B, C, D, E = mean

Each pillar is the average of its available data points. Higher A and B mean more risk. Higher C, D and E mean more readiness.

Build the two sides

Weights are powers, so one very weak pillar cannot be hidden by a strong one.

Discount for equity

Readiness that does not reach everyone counts for less. E never adds readiness; it only keeps or cuts it.

Score 0 to 100

WSFI = 100 × Risk ÷ (Risk + Ready)

The same as gap ÷ (1 + gap), where gap = Risk ÷ Ready.

50 has a meaningRisk equals effective readiness. Above 50, risk is ahead, which matches the design's "priority country" line.
No league-table effectThe score is bounded by its own formula, not by the best and worst country in the sample.
Weights that add upA and B share the risk side (60/40), C and D share the readiness side (60/40), keeping the framework's 30/20/30/20 proportions.
